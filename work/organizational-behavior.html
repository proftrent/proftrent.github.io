MG-GY 6013: Organizational Behavior

MG-GY 6013: Organizational Behavior

How do you preserve the social energy of an interaction-heavy course when moving it online?

For NYU Tandon’s graduate Organizational Behavior course, we created a distributed learning environment spanning interactive, media-rich Rise modules, Google Community conversations, weekly virtual teamwork, and synchronous digital simulations. And students noticed, specifically shouting out the innovative approaches to delivering such an interaction-heavy course through a virtual, mostly asynchronous classroom. But the students weren't the only ones to notice, as our work received the NYU Tandon Online Course Development Team Award for excellence and innovation in development.

AT A GLANCE

Context
Educational TechnologistNew York University2018
Audience
Graduate Management of Technology students
Scale

50+ students

Collaborators

Anne-Laure Fayard · Professor of Innovation, Design and Organizational Studies

Jay Leibowitz · Instructional Designer

Need

Translate a highly social, activity-driven classroom course into a primarily asynchronous experience without losing peer interaction, experimentation, or immediacy.

Solution

For asynchronous learning and live session preparation, develop interactive, media-rich Rise modules integrating Google Community discussions and custom Qualtrics activities. For live virtual sessions, use weekly team meetings and simulations to practice teamwork, leadership, and change management.

Outcome

After its inaugural run in Fall 2018, the course received the NYU Tandon Online Course Development Team Award for excellence and innovation. It then went on to be offered at least three more times over the next four years.

Impact

Students in the intial offering rated the course exceptionally highly, particularly praising the online materials, technology, and how they supported peer and instructor interactions.

Takeaway

This was my first course developed in close collaboration with others, and it showed me both the value and the limits of the creative problem-solving I had cultivated as an adjunct. I learned to trade some ambitious ideas for simpler solutions that faculty and students with different levels of technical confidence could adopt, sustain, and scale. My university teaching experience also helped me earn the trust of a faculty partner who was initially skeptical of online learning—and ultimately demonstrate that the course could meet her expectations without losing what made the classroom experience work.

Methods & Tools: Articulate Rise · Qualtrics · Google Community · Simulations: Mount Everest & Change Pro · Learning Glass
