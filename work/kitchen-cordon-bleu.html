Kitchen Cordon Bleu

Kitchen Cordon Bleu

What should an accessible alternative preserve—and what might it become when accessibility shapes the design from the beginning?

When the planned VR inventory audit could not equitably serve a learner with a severe visual impairment, I designed and developed a screen-reader-compatible simulation from scratch in Articulate Storyline. Kitchen Cordon Bleu preserved the professional performance at the heart of the assignment—investigating inventory, documenting evidence, and advising a client—while becoming a different kind of experience: a richly explorable desktop warehouse with characters, competing clues, two audit directions, and multiple levels of support. It served as the accessible companion to the larger ACT 4210: Auditing I redesign, but was substantial enough that any student could choose it.

AT A GLANCE

Context
Senior Instructional DesignerNova Southeastern University2020–2021
Audience
Undergraduate accounting students
Scale

240 inspectable boxes

2 modes · 2 audit directions

Collaborator

Linda Kidwell · Professor of Accounting

Need

Create an equitable alternative to a headset-based inventory audit without replacing authentic practice with a passive or diminished assignment.

Solution

A screen-reader-compatible Storyline simulation where junior auditors explore a fictional company, inspect inventory, question employees, complete workpapers, and recommend action.

Outcome

A standalone experience supporting JAWS and NVDA, with guided and advanced modes, list-to-floor and floor-to-list auditing, randomized sampling, and an explorable narrative environment.

Impact

Students could choose the desktop experience instead of—or in addition to—VR. Survey respondents using it rated navigation, box inspection, and several core workpaper actions exceptionally highly.

Takeaway

Accessibility inspired a richer product, but richness needed restraint. The simulation proved that an equitable alternative can stand on its own; student performance also showed me that narrative detail earns its place only when it sharpens the professional task rather than competing with it.

Methods & Tools: Articulate Storyline · Simulation architecture · Narrative design · JAWS · NVDA · Qualtrics
